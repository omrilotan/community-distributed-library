How it works

Things worth owning together

Books

The novel you finished last month is somebody's next read.

Tools

A drill spends most of its life in a drawer. Let it work.

Board games

Game night without buying another box that fits nowhere.

Camping gear

A tent for the one weekend a year you actually use it.

Ask, meet, hand over, bring back

  1. List what you are happy to lend

    Add anything else with a name, photo, description and tags. It stays on your shelf until somebody asks.

  2. Ask a neighbour

    Browse what is near you, ask for it, and talk it through in a thread. No money changes hands.

  3. Meet and hand it over

    Pick a time both your calendars allow, meet, and confirm with a code. Bringing it back works the same way.

Fewer things bought. More neighbours known.

Community

Every loan is a doorstep conversation. A library of a few hundred people is small enough that faces become names, and names become the people you call when the boiler goes.

Sustainability

The greenest product is the one nobody had to make. A street that shares its tools and tents buys less, throws away less, and keeps what it has in use.

Built for people who live near each other

  • Invitation only

    A neighbour lets you in. Nobody arrives from a search engine.

  • Lent, never rented

    Nothing costs anything. Things go out and come back.

  • Your own rules

    Each library sets its loan length and who may invite.

  • Face to face

    Handoffs happen in person, at a time you both picked.

  • Wait your turn

    Something out on loan? Join the queue and hear when it is back.

  • Works with assistants

    An AI assistant can browse the shelf and ask on your behalf.

Everyone welcome

There are no ratings here, no reviews and no stars, for items or for people. That is on purpose. A library like this runs on trust between neighbours, and every loan is already a kindness.
