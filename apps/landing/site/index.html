Ask, meet, hand over, bring back

  1. List what you are happy to lend

    Add anything else with a name, photo, description and tags. It stays on your shelf until somebody asks.

  2. Ask a neighbour

    Browse what is near you, ask for it, and talk it through in a thread. No money changes hands.

  3. Meet and hand it over

    Pick a time both your calendars allow, meet, and confirm with a code. Bringing it back works the same way.

Fewer things bought. More neighbours known.

Community

Every loan is a doorstep conversation. A library of a few hundred people is small enough that faces become names, and names become the people you call when the boiler goes.

Sustainability

The greenest product is the one nobody had to make. A street that shares its tools and tents buys less, throws away less, and keeps what it has in use.

Built for people who live near each other

A look inside

It runs in the browser of the phone you already have, with no app store in between. Add it to your home screen and it opens like an app, with its own icon, and tells you when someone asks for something or answers you. If you have not opened it within five minutes, or have notifications turned off, the same news arrives by email, so nothing slips past.

These are the screens of a library of things, from finding a sewing machine to handing over a badminton set.

Everyone welcome

There are no ratings here, no reviews and no stars, for items or for people. That is on purpose. A library like this runs on trust between neighbours, and every loan is already a kindness.

Looked after by neighbours

Every library can have as many moderators as it needs: neighbours who lend it a little of their time. Most days there is nothing for them to do. They are there for the days when there is.

  • Keeping the shelves in order

    They sort what neighbours list: tagging things so they are easy to find, turning a suggestion into a new category, and putting right a name or a photo for everyone.

  • When a loan goes sideways

    A report goes to a person, never to an algorithm. A moderator reads it, writes back and helps put it right. A loan that is long overdue reaches them too.

  • Answerable, too

    They read a private conversation only once it has been reported. Everything they change is logged for the library's admin, and a complaint about a moderator goes to the admin, never to the moderators.

Ask your assistant for anything

Every library comes with its own MCP server. Connect Claude, ChatGPT or any assistant that speaks MCP, sign in once, and then do everything in your own words. No forms, no menus.

  • Find and ask

    “Is anyone lending a ladder this weekend?” It searches the shelf and asks the neighbour for you.

  • List what you own

    Tell it what is on your shelf, or show it a photo, and it adds each one with its cover and details.

  • Lend and arrange

    It answers requests, writes to the borrower, extends a loan or puts you in the queue.

  • On your terms

    You choose what it may do when you connect, and it checks with you before it writes to anyone.

How to connect one

Start one for your street

The software is open source and in the public domain. Anyone can run a library for their own neighbourhood, on their own address, with nobody in between. It runs on a Cloudflare account on the Workers Paid plan (US$5 a month), which covers its email too: nothing else to sign up for.

It is one project that runs any number of libraries, of books or of things. Each library is its own small deployment, with its own members, its own data and its own rules, and no central service that could close, sell or change it. Use it, change it, share it: the code is yours without asking. Ideas, fixes and translations are welcome on GitHub.

  • Let an AI assistant set it up

    Open the code in Claude Code and type /open-a-library. The assistant checks your computer, walks you through the one key it needs, creates the library and makes you its first admin. No programming needed.

  • You stay in charge

    Your keys go straight into a file on your computer, never into the chat, and the assistant asks before it creates anything in your account.

  • Keep it up to date

    Type /update-libraries to bring your library the latest version, or /make-admin to hand someone a role.
